DEPARTMENT OF ORTHOPAEDIC SURGERY · MAHARAT NAKHON RATCHASIMA HOSPITAL
A training portfolio that writes itself while we work
Training already happens every day — cases, presentations, teaching, assessment. What costs us time is not the work. It is retyping later what we already did.
This system captures it as it happens, and assembles the portfolio for us.

WHY RECORDS END UP LATE AND THIN
Four things that cost us time today
Recorded later means half-remembered
What was the operation two months ago, and who supervised? The later we record, the less accurate it is — and the less we want to do it.
Evidence scattered
The slide deck someone presented sits on their own laptop. At assessment time we scroll a LINE group backwards through a whole year.
The roster lives in people's heads
Who is on which team, which theatre today, who swapped with whom. Verbal only — so nobody can confirm who actually supervised.
Work piles up at inspection
WFME evidence and personal files are assembled just before review, even though the data was produced all year.
ONE IDEA
Capture at the moment, assemble later
Nothing here asks you to fill in a new form about work you already did. Every function either takes data that already exists somewhere else, or records a decision at the moment it is made.
Already exists
Operative cases come from the OR queue. Academic activity comes from the slide file you just presented.
Decided now
Assessment at the end of the session. A day swap when it is agreed. Which theatre you actually went to, on the day.
Assembled for you
Progress against requirements, EPA levels, WFME evidence and the printable personal file are built from the two on the left.
ROLES
Three roles, three different views
Permission is enforced in the data layer, not by hiding buttons. A resident sees only their own record — even calling the function directly returns nothing.
Resident — own portfolio, logbook, EPA; shared rosters
Attending — every resident's record, to assess and certify
Programme director — registers, rotations, requirements, all data
HOW TO USE
1. Open the link, pick your account from the list
2. Expand demo codes and press fill in the code
3. Add to Home Screen on your phone — it then works offline
LANDING PAGE ON A PHONE
Where do I go now, what is waiting on me
Sessions in clock order — timed ones first, all-day theatre behind them. Academic sessions are flagged as taking priority. Clashes the system refuses to decide for you are called out at the top.
HOW TO USE
1. Open the app — residents land here automatically
2. Read the top banners: academic activity, and any clash you must settle
3. Tap a session to record which one you attended, and who supervised
4. ‹ › moves day by day; it returns to today when reopened
LANDING PAGE ON A PHONE
Your sessions, your inbox, your overdue assessments
Only sessions that name you, with the residents in each — tap a name to assess. Requests waiting on you sit above: day swaps to accept, out-of-team requests to approve.
HOW TO USE
1. At the end of a session, open the app and tap the resident's name
2. Accept or decline swap requests — the roster does not change until you accept
3. Approve out-of-team requests from residents you supervise
4. Overdue assessments are counted separately from other attendings'
LANDING PAGE
The department at a glance, and what is missing
Who is on which team today, how many sessions are running, and the gaps that matter: residents with no session at all, rotations with no dates, and requests still waiting on someone.
HOW TO USE
1. Scan the team cards for a team with nobody on it
2. Check the counters for pending approvals and overdue assessments
3. Jump to the roster or the coverage view from the buttons below
CAPTURE
Drop the slide file you just presented
The file is read in the browser — it never leaves the machine. The system pulls the title, author and date, reads the slide text, and proposes the activity type and subspecialty, saying which words it matched and how confident it is.
Stored slide text is stripped of anything that looks like a patient identifier first.
HOW TO USE
1. Straight after presenting, drag the .pptx or .pdf onto the drop zone
2. Check the proposed type and subspecialty; correct if wrong
3. Press confirm — the evidence now has the real file behind it
FOUR LAYERS
The weekly roster is built, not typed
A day's sessions come from four layers multiplied by the rotation plan: department-wide sessions everyone attends, the unit you rotate on, the team you still belong to, and the monthly duty roster.
Subspecialty units have no roster of their own — the work is divided along the teams.
HOW TO USE
1. Write each team's weekly pattern once; it applies all year
2. Set the rotation blocks; the calendar comes from the two
3. Read across a row to see one resident's week
WHEN TWO SESSIONS COLLIDE
Priority decides — except when it should not
Academic activity outranks everything, so an R4 goes to the inter-hospital conference even when it collides with theatre. The losing session stays visible and says what it lost to.
When both sides have equal priority the system does not decide — it marks both and asks.
HOW TO USE
1. Open the session that clashes
2. Press record that I attended this one and name the supervisor
3. That applies to that date only — the year's rule is untouched
MONTHLY
What does not repeat weekly
Daily ER attending, which team covers ER, Thursday clinic, and public holidays. Fill the month from the weekly pattern, then change only the days that differ.
Tick a holiday and every routine session that day disappears, leaving only what the duty roster itself states.
HOW TO USE
1. Press fill from the weekly pattern at the start of the month
2. Edit only the exceptions
3. Tick public holidays — the calendar clears them
ATTENDINGS
A swap is a request, not an announcement
One attending asks, the other accepts. Until it is accepted the roster still shows the original name, with a note that a request is pending. Once accepted it reads “X (covering for Y)” — never a silent rename.
It applies to that date only. Editing the weekly pattern instead would change every other week of the year and rewrite history.
HOW TO USE
1. Press request a swap on your session, or use the roster page
2. Choose who you are asking, and the date you cover in return
3. They accept from their Today page — the return half is created then
ALWAYS NEEDS APPROVAL
Working outside your own team
A case belonging to your research, a rarely seen case, a procedure you still owe, or a short-handed day where the chief resident redeploys you.
All of them need the supervising attending's approval. The chief can move people on the day, but signing off where someone worked belongs to an attending. Before approval the session does not exist in the calendar at all.
HOW TO USE
1. Pick the date and the session
2. Give the reason — research, rare case, requirements, chief's assignment
3. Your supervising attending approves it from their Today page
4. Filing afterwards is allowed and is flagged as retrospective
THE FLAGSHIP CHANGE
One assessment, both records
The end-of-session form and the EPA assessment use the same 1–5 entrustment scale. They used to be two separate jobs — the second carried no new judgement, so it got skipped, and EPA levels stayed empty for training that had actually happened.
Now the form lists the EPA topics, subspecialty matches first. One save writes both, and the EPA level records which session it came from.
HOW TO USE
1. Score the three domains and set the entrustment level
2. Tick the EPA topics the session covered — starred ones match
3. Save once. Editing overwrites; deleting removes what it created
OPERATIVE CASES
Cases arrive from the OR queue
Date, diagnosis, operation and surgeon come across from the queue. The resident only confirms their role. The queue does not record assistants yet, so that step stays manual.
A resident cannot certify their own case. Patient name is never imported, even though the queue holds it — how much else is stored is a department setting, down to nothing at all.
HOW TO USE
1. Press fetch from the queue, or import a file when off the intranet
2. Open a case and tick who took part, in which role
3. An attending certifies the roles
ENTRUSTMENT
Levels per topic against the year's target
Each topic shows the latest level, the target for that year, and the history of who assessed it and when. Levels written from a session say which session they came from.
The six topics in the system are a scaffold, not the curriculum's real list — we need the real one.
HOW TO USE
1. Pick the resident (residents see only themselves)
2. Read the matrix — red means below the year's target
3. Assess here for topics that belong to no single session
INSTITUTIONAL REVIEW
Evidence tied to its standard as it is produced
Activities, assessments and cases are linked to the WFME areas they evidence at the time they happen, so the report can be printed at any moment instead of assembled in the weeks before a review.
Wording is drawn from the nine-area structure of the 2015 revision and has not been checked line by line against the document used for submission.
HOW TO USE
1. Open the area you need evidence for
2. Check what is linked and what is thin
3. Print the report
PER RESIDENT
Progress, gaps, and a page to sign
Progress against this year's requirements, mean scores, entrustment, and — most usefully — what is still missing while there is time to fix it, rather than at the end of the year.
HOW TO USE
1. Open your own file (attendings and the director can open anyone's)
2. Work down the gaps list
3. Print it with the signature block for the formal review

SAID PLAINLY
What this is not, yet
No central database yet
Data lives in each browser. Everyone opening the link gets their own demo copy. Not yet shared use.
Sign-in is not real authentication
It separates views on a shared machine. Real deployment must bind to hospital accounts and check on the server.
Does not replace the College logbook
The official record stays where it is. This prepares the data and catches what is missing.
What it does not reduce
Judging how well a resident performs is still the attending's work. This removes retyping, not judgement.
